OUR MISSION

To teach students how to use artificial intelligence properly, honestly and in a way that leaves them more capable than it found them. We do that two ways. We build one careful worked example, TUTORial, so you can see what a defensible use of AI actually looks like. And we give away a free multi unit curriculum, {{ unit_count }} units and {{ exercise_count }} interactive exercises, that teaches the method itself.

TUTORIAL IS NOT THE BEST USE OF AI

We want to be plain about this, because plenty of products in this space are not. TUTORial is not the highest and best thing artificial intelligence can do. It is not going to cure a disease or read a genome. It is one narrow tool that turns a photograph of a problem into a video that stops and makes you do the work.

What it is, is an example. It is a use of AI that augments a learner rather than replacing one, that keeps a human accountable for every answer, that is honest about where the machine can be wrong, and that can be checked. Those four properties are the whole lesson. If you take one thing from this site, take the properties and leave the product.

WHY OURS BEATS “SHOW ME A SIMILAR PROBLEM”

A chatbot answers by handing you a finished worked solution to read. We hand you a segmented video that stops and makes you produce each step. Fifty years of memory and instructional research says those two things are not close. Parenthetical citations point to the works cited page.

RETRIEVAL STICKS. RE READING EVAPORATES.
Learners who practised retrieving material retained roughly 50% more a week later than learners who reviewed the same content by studying it again. Reading a solution is the review condition. Answering a checkpoint is the retrieval condition.
(Karpicke and Blunt 772 to 775, Dunlosky et al. 4 to 58)
THE ONE SENTENCE VERSION
A similar problem is a worked example with no retrieval, no segmentation, no second channel, no error check and no proof you understood it. Our dynamic video is a worked example that fades into retrieval exactly where the research says fading works, which is why it beats the chat answer on retention, on transfer, under exam pressure, and for the learners a wall of text leaves behind.
